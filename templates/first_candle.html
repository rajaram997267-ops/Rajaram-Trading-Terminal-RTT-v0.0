Top movers study

Which alerts end the day among the F&O stocks' biggest gainers (for Buy alerts) or losers (for Sell alerts), and what do they have in common? It uses the days loaded by the Benchmark above, so press Build benchmark first, then Run study. Chance = how often a random F&O stock would land in the top N. Lift = hits ÷ what chance alone would give (1.0× = no better than random).

Recipe tester

Test one exact recipe: pick the alert type(s), then switch on the conditions you want. Press Run study first (it loads the days). The funnel adds one condition at a time so you can see which one helps and which one only shrinks the sample. Conditions marked (end of day) are only known after the close, so they cannot be used to decide an entry at 9:20.
Alerts (none ticked = any):
The combinations table uses the factors ticked above (tick "alert name" to split each combination by the alert that fired). Day volume and sector strength are only known at the end of the day, so they help explain what makes movers but cannot be used to decide an entry at 9:20 (sector strength = the sector's average move without the stock itself, minus the market's median move; ticking it makes the combinations much smaller, so use it with a lower minimum or more days). "Avg move" follows the "Measure move" choice in the Benchmark. Rows with fewer stocks than the minimum are greyed out or hidden - small samples are mostly luck.
Covers stocks with a Buy or Sell alert on the chosen day. 9:15 ratio = first 5-min candle volume ÷ its average over the previous 10 sessions. Day vol = whole-day volume so far ÷ the average at the same clock time. Below 20d high = previous close vs the highest high of the last 20 sessions. After 9:15 % = move from the 9:15 candle's close to the latest close - the part you could actually trade (the benchmark uses this by default; "from previous close" also counts the overnight gap). "Best swing" is the peak after 9:15, so it is optimistic. 9:15 buy share (the benchmark can group by it - for a Sell alert it uses the selling share) and Dir. pressure estimate buying vs selling from where the candle closed in its range (not true buy/sell volume); Dir. pressure is the side matching the alert (buying for Buy, selling for Sell) vs its 10-session average. A day still in progress shows partial numbers. Click a column heading to sort.
